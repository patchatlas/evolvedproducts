Washer fluid and freezing weather: what to know before winter

In places like South Dakota, the first hard freeze can show up overnight. If your washer reservoir is still full of summer fluid, you might find out the hard way when you pull the lever and nothing comes out.

Why summer fluid freezes

Summer and bug-wash formulas are mostly water with cleaners, and many contain little or no antifreeze. Water freezes at 32°F. When the fluid freezes it expands, which can block the lines and nozzles. In some cases it can also strain the reservoir or the washer pump.

Read the label

Winter washer fluids are labeled with a temperature rating, such as −20°F. Pick a rating that matches the coldest weather you expect to drive in, not just the average.

How to switch over

  1. Before the first freeze, use the washers until the reservoir is low.
  2. Fill the reservoir with a winter-rated fluid.
  3. Run the washers for a few seconds to pull the winter fluid through the lines and nozzles.

If it's already frozen

Park somewhere warm, like a heated garage, and let it thaw before you try the washers. Running the pump against frozen fluid can damage it. Once it thaws, replace the fluid with a winter-rated one.

Don't forget wipers

Winter is hard on wiper blades too. If they streak or chatter, replace them so the fluid can do its job.

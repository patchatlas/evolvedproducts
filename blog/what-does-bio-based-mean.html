What does "bio-based" mean on a product label?

You'll see more and more products labeled "bio-based," "plant-based" or "biodegradable." These words sound alike, but they mean different things.

Bio-based

Bio-based describes what a product is made from. The USDA describes biobased products as products made from plants and other renewable agricultural, marine and forestry materials, instead of petroleum.

Biodegradable

Biodegradable describes what happens to a product after use: whether, and how fast, natural processes break it down. A product can be bio-based without being biodegradable, and the other way around. Claims about biodegradability are supposed to be backed by testing.

USDA BioPreferred®

The USDA BioPreferred® Program runs a voluntary label for biobased products. A company applies, and the product's biobased content is tested by an independent lab. Only products that pass can carry the USDA Certified Biobased Product label, which shows the percentage of biobased content.

How to read a label

  • Look for specific numbers, such as a certified biobased percentage, instead of vague words.
  • Check that a certification logo is real and current. The USDA lists certified products on its website.
  • Remember that "bio-based" alone doesn't tell you how a product performs. Read the directions and ratings, like freeze temperature for washer fluid.
